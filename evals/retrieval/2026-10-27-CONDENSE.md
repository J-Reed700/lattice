# Condensing a research turn's results: one pass against one per result (2026-10-27)

Local Qwen3.5-9B Q4_K_M, one slot, llama.cpp build 11669 on an RTX 4070 Laptop GPU, the app's cache flags; a
16,384-token window, then 65,536 for the packed turns. Code: `chat/tool_loop/research_notes.rs`.

## Why

When a turn's tool results pass 60% of the window, one request rewrites all of them into notes that must keep
every fact and citation number. The same model loses facts when it reads many passages at once (the reading,
before it went to groups of four), so one pass over everything a turn has read was suspected of the same.

## Method

`scripts/replay_condense_ab.py`. The saved reading prompts' passages stand in for what a research turn has read:
the question and instructions stay in the message, and the passages come back as the results of the turn's
calls (`semantic_search` for the question, then `get_document` for the section a result opens with). Every
request is the shipped condensing request: the instruction said after the last result, the same tools
forbidden, deterministic sampling, thinking off, the round's reservation.

- **ONE**: as shipped, every result in one request.
- **EACH**: one request per result, the result shown again after the instruction.
- **NAMED**: one request per result, naming its call only.
- **ONE-USER**: ONE with the instruction as a user message of its own.
- **ONE-BARE**: ONE without the tools.

Each reply is taken as `usable_notes` takes it: the text before any call written into it, and nothing when it was
cut off or holds nothing else. An unusable reply leaves its results as they were, as the app carries on without
condensing them, so it loses nothing and saves no room. A fact is kept when the transcript after condensing holds
one of the rubric's spellings for it; it keeps its citation when its passage's label is on its line or the last
one cited before it.

## Results

**16k window**, 28 turns (16 long questions, six facts each; 12 short, one or two), about 23,000 characters of
results each, four passages to a result:

| Arm | Facts kept | with citation | Usable replies | Replies writing a call | After / before | Median s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ONE** | **112/112** | 109 | 22/28 | 6 | 0.23 | **6.5** |
| EACH | 112/112 | 112 | 89/140 | 51 | 0.43 | 20.1 |
| NAMED | 112/112 | 110 | 97/140 | 43 | 0.45 | 17.4 |
| ONE-USER | 112/112 | 111 | **28/28** | 0 | **0.06** | 11.5 |
| ONE-BARE | 112/112 | 108 | 23/28 | 5 | 0.20 | 10.7 |

**64k window**, six packed turns: four long questions asked together, 24 facts and about 120,000 characters of
results (some 30,000 tokens) in one condensing, eight passages to a result:

| Arm | Facts kept | with citation | Usable replies | After / before | Median s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ONE** | **144/144** | **143** | 6/6 | 0.03 | **28.8** |
| ONE-USER | 144/144 | 140 | 6/6 | 0.03 | 52.7 |
| EACH (first turn only, 436 s) | 24/24 | 23 | 14/14 | 0.38 | 435.6 |

- One pass lost no fact at either size. Its notes keep each fact's meaning and conditions, one line per fact with
  its citation ("Logs for the Peallby stove must read under 41.6 per cent moisture on the meter, or the glass
  blackens within a day [4]."), and condensed 719,060 characters to 18,873 at 64k.
- One per result is slower, condenses less, and writes calls more often.
- ONE's only failure: on six short-question turns (of 12) the reply was a tool call, not notes, so nothing was
  condensed. Said as a message of its own (ONE-USER), the instruction got notes on all six. Without tools
  (ONE-BARE) the model still wrote calls on five. ONE-USER costs a read of the whole prompt (a new user message
  changes how Qwen 3.5's template renders the turns before it), about 5 s at 16k and 24 s at 64k, and kept three
  fewer citations at 64k.

## Decision

Keep one pass, said after the last result. When its reply is a call instead of notes, ask once more with the
instruction as a message of its own (`condense`), which pays the re-read only in the case that fails today.

## What this does not show

The facts are planted details among unrelated text: notes have to find them, not choose among many facts that
all bear on the question, as dense web pages would ask. Facts are scored by their spelling, not their meaning
(read by hand in samples). Every run is the 9B.
