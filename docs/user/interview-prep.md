# Prepare for an interview in a day

This is the shortest path through Studio from "I have an interview tomorrow"
to answers you can give from memory under pressure. Each step uses a
technique with good evidence behind it; the
[design note](../design/2026-10-26-interview-prep-and-study-sessions.md) has
the research.

## 1. Set up (about 20 minutes)

1. **Import your material.** Add the job description, your résumé and any
   notes about the company to the Library (**Import**).
2. **Make a program.** In **Studio**, create a program such as "Prepare for
   the Backend Engineer interview at Acme", then accept its outline.
3. **Make the interview.** Open the program, go to **Practice → Code &
   simulations → New activity**, and choose **Interview**. Fill in the
   company, its website, the role and the round, and paste the job
   description into the notes. Generate it: Lattice researches the company
   and writes the questions with a private answer guide for each.

## 2. Try it cold once

Start the mock interview and answer every question before you study anything.
Getting answers wrong now is useful: an attempt before the answer makes the
answer stick better once you see it. Finish the simulation to read the
feedback.

## 3. Make the drill

Under the interview, choose **Make drill deck** and enter the interview's
date and time. Every question becomes a card, and its answer guide is shown
only after you have answered. The deck opens with your plan for the day.

You can also make decks from the job description and your notes
(**Studio → Flashcards → New deck**), then set the same date on each with
**Set date**.

## 4. Run each session

For every card:

1. **Answer aloud** as you would in the room, or type an outline. Aim for the
   time shown.
2. **Say how sure you are.** That reveals the guide.
3. **Tick the points you covered.** Lattice suggests a rating from them. Be
   honest: a miss now is cheap.
4. **Rate it.** A miss comes back later in the same session until you get it.
   If you were sure and wrong, read that correction carefully: those are the
   errors you are most likely to fix.

Number keys work too: 1-3 for how sure you are, then 1-4 to rate.

## 5. Follow the plan

- **First pass now.**
- **Again when cards come due.** The deck shows when. Hours apart works better
  than drilling straight on.
- **Once before you sleep.** Sleep after practice consolidates it.
- **A ten-minute warm-up two hours before.** Say your key answers and stories
  aloud. Nothing new.
- **A final mock interview** if there is time, then stop. Rest, read your notes
  once, and arrive early.
